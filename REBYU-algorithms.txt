================================================================================
PART 1 - BKT (BAYESIAN KNOWLEDGE TRACING)
================================================================================

Corbett & Anderson (1995). Tracks the probability a learner knows one lesson.

File: python-backend/app/services/bkt_math.py   ->  update_mastery()


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FOUR PARAMETERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  prior   0.30    assumed chance of knowing it before any evidence
  guess   0.25    chance of answering RIGHT without knowing (4-option MCQ)
  slip    0.10    chance of answering WRONG while knowing (misread, typo)
  learn   0.08    chance of picking it up by attempting the question
  forget  0.0     chance of losing it (unused)

Defined in  python-backend/app/core/config.py:186  as fallback_*
Mirrored in backend-java/.../adaptive/config/AdaptiveProperties.java

guess and slip are the whole point. A right answer might be luck. A wrong
answer might be carelessness. Without them the update would just be
"right = up, wrong = down".


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1 - BAYES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWERED CORRECTLY

                        p * (1 - slip)
    posterior = -------------------------------------
                 p * (1 - slip) + (1 - p) * guess

    "Of all the ways you could have answered correctly,
     what share involved actually knowing it?"


ANSWERED WRONGLY

                          p * slip
    posterior = -------------------------------------
                 p * slip + (1 - p) * (1 - guess)

    "Of all the ways you could have got it wrong,
     what share involved knowing it anyway?"


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2 - LEARNING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    mastery_after = posterior * (1 - forget) + (1 - posterior) * learn

Even a wrong answer nudges mastery up slightly, because attempting a question
teaches you something.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PARTIAL CREDIT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    weight    = score, if given, else (1 if correct else 0)
    posterior = weight * posterior_right + (1 - weight) * posterior_wrong

A half-marked diagram is half the evidence of a right answer, instead of being
rounded to one or the other. This matters for diagrams and code, which rarely
score 0 or 100.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ALSO COMPUTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    predicted_correct = p * (1 - slip) + (1 - p) * guess

The model's prediction before it sees the answer.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLES - starting at p = 0.30
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CORRECT ANSWER

    top       = 0.30 * 0.90 = 0.27
    bottom    = 0.27 + (0.70 * 0.25) = 0.445
    posterior = 0.27 / 0.445 = 0.61
    mastery   = 0.61 + (0.39 * 0.08) = 0.64

    0.30  ->  0.64


WRONG ANSWER

    top       = 0.30 * 0.10 = 0.03
    bottom    = 0.03 + (0.70 * 0.75) = 0.555
    posterior = 0.03 / 0.555 = 0.054
    mastery   = 0.054 + (0.946 * 0.08) = 0.13

    0.30  ->  0.13


HALF MARKS (score = 0.5)

    posterior = 0.5 * 0.61 + 0.5 * 0.054 = 0.33
    mastery   = 0.33 + (0.67 * 0.08) = 0.38

    0.30  ->  0.38


A wrong answer moves mastery FURTHER than a right one. That is correct, not a
bug: with a 25% guess rate one right answer is weak evidence, while with only a
10% slip rate a wrong answer is strong evidence.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MASTERY BANDS       python-backend/app/core/config.py:206
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    0.85 and above    mastered
    0.70 and above    good
    0.40 and above    developing
    below 0.40        weak


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE IT IS STORED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

python-backend/app/services/mastery_service.py

One row per (learner, lesson) in bkt.learner_lesson_mastery, read with
with_for_update() so two answers arriving at once cannot overwrite each other.


================================================================================
PART 2 - IRT (ITEM RESPONSE THEORY, 3PL)
================================================================================

Three-parameter logistic model with a Bayesian ability update.

File: backend-java/src/main/java/com/capstone/rebyu/adaptive/engine/IrtModel.java


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ITEM PARAMETERS        record ItemParams(double a, double b, double c)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  a    discrimination - how sharply the item separates strong from weak
       range 0.2 to 4.0, default 1.0 (higher for critical-thinking items)

  b    difficulty, on the same scale as theta
       range -4.0 to 4.0
            EASY     -1.5
            AVERAGE   0.0
            HARD     +1.5

  c    guessing floor
       range 0.0 to 0.5, default 0.0


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ABILITY SCALE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    THETA_MIN       = -3.0
    THETA_MAX       =  3.0
    THETA_BASELINE  =  0.0     every learner starts here
    PRIOR_SIGMA     =  1.7     starting uncertainty
    MIN_SIGMA       =  0.75    uncertainty never drops below this


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FORMULA 1 - PROBABILITY OF A CORRECT ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

                                1
    p = c + (1 - c) * ----------------------
                       1 + e^(-a(theta - b))

A logistic S-curve. Only theta - b matters: ability minus difficulty.

    theta - b = -1.5   ->   p = 0.18
    theta - b =  0.0   ->   p = 0.50
    theta - b = +1.5   ->   p = 0.82


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FORMULA 2 - FISHER INFORMATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

                      q     ( p - c )^2
    information = a^2 * - * ( ------- )           where q = 1 - p
                      p     ( 1 - c )

INFORMATION PEAKS AT p = 0.5.

A question the learner would get right half the time is maximally informative,
because the outcome is genuinely uncertain. A question they would certainly
pass, or certainly fail, tells us nothing.

This is why the exam adapts: AdaptiveItemSelector serves the unseen item with
the highest information at the learner's current theta.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FORMULA 3 - THE ABILITY UPDATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    gradient = a * (u - p) * (p - c) / (p * (1 - c))

               with c = 0 this is simply   a * (u - p)


                      variance
    gain     = --------------------------         variance = sigma^2
                1 + variance * information


    theta_new = clamp(theta + gain * gradient, -3, +3)


                                1
    sigma_new = max(0.75, sqrt( ------------------------- ))
                                 1/variance + information


THREE IDEAS

    u - p      surprise. The learner scored u, the model expected p.
               The bigger the gap, the bigger the move.

    gain       how far to move. High while uncertainty is high,
               shrinking as sigma falls.

    sigma      uncertainty. Only ever decreases, floored at 0.75 so the
               model never stops responding to new evidence.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FORMULA 4 - STANDARD ERROR ACROSS A WHOLE ATTEMPT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

              1
    total = ------- + sum of information(theta, item) for every item
             1.7^2

                             1
    SE    = max(0.75, ---------------- )
                       sqrt(total)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROFICIENCY RATING - what the learner sees
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    rating = ((theta - THETA_MIN) / (THETA_MAX - THETA_MIN)) * 100
           = ((theta + 3) / 6) * 100

    theta -3.0   ->   rating 0     Novice
    theta -1.5   ->   rating 25    Developing
    theta  0.0   ->   rating 50    Proficient   <- the pass line
    theta +1.5   ->   rating 75    Advanced
    theta +3.0   ->   rating 100   Advanced

    rating 75 and above   Advanced
    rating 50 and above   Proficient
    rating 25 and above   Developing
    below 25              Novice

A FORFEITED attempt returns 0, whatever theta said while it was running.
A NON-ADAPTIVE attempt (diagnostic, mock) has no proficiency rating at all,
only a percentage.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLE - one question from theta = 0
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    QUESTION             p        information    theta becomes
    ----------------------------------------------------------
    Average, correct     0.500      0.250           +0.839
    Hard, correct        0.182      0.149           +1.651
    Easy, wrong          0.818      0.149           -1.651

A SURPRISING answer moves theta roughly twice as far as an expected one.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLE - five correct average answers in a row
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    answer 1:    theta +0.839    sigma 1.295    rating 64
    answer 2:    theta +1.213    sigma 1.113    rating 70
    answer 3:    theta +1.446    sigma 1.008    rating 74
    answer 4:    theta +1.614    sigma 0.938    rating 77
    answer 5:    theta +1.744    sigma 0.885    rating 79

The steps shrink:  +0.84,  +0.37,  +0.23,  +0.17,  +0.13

As sigma falls, gain falls with it, so the estimate converges instead of
running away.


================================================================================
PART 3 - HOW THE TWO MEET
================================================================================

    learner answers a question
            |
            +---> IRT    updates theta for this attempt      (Java, instant)
            |            -> picks the next question
            |            -> proficiency rating at the end
            |
            +---> BKT    outbox event -> Python service      (background)
                         -> updates mastery for that lesson
                         -> feeds study plan order, priorities, readiness


IRT is synchronous because the next question depends on it.

BKT travels through a transactional outbox (bkt_event_outbox) so a slow or
unavailable Python service never blocks a learner's submit, and no mastery
update is ever lost.

Both sides use the same parameter defaults: Java's AdaptiveProperties and
Python's fallback_* settings hold identical values.


================================================================================
LIKELY PANEL QUESTIONS
================================================================================

Q: Why two models?
A: They answer different questions. BKT is per-lesson and durable - it tells
   the study plan which topics are weak. IRT is per-attempt and momentary - it
   tells the engine which question to serve next and produces the proficiency
   rating.

Q: Why not a simple percentage score?
A: Because the quiz adapts. Scoring 50% on hard items and 50% on easy items are
   not the same achievement, and a raw percentage cannot tell them apart. Theta
   accounts for the difficulty of the items actually served.

Q: Why does a wrong answer hurt more than a right answer helps in BKT?
A: Because guess (0.25) is larger than slip (0.10). A right answer could be a
   lucky guess; a wrong answer is much less likely to be a slip, so it is
   stronger evidence.

Q: What stops theta running away?
A: Gain falls as sigma falls, so each answer moves theta less than the last,
   and theta is clamped to plus or minus 3. Sigma is floored at 0.75 so the
   model never becomes so confident that it stops responding.

Q: What about partially correct answers?
A: BKT blends the two posteriors by the score. IRT takes u as a 0-to-1 score
   rather than a boolean. Both handle a diagram marked 7 out of 10 without
   rounding it.

Q: Are the parameters fitted per lesson?
A: Not currently. BktParameterProvider always returns the defaults. Per-lesson
   parameter fitting is built but switched off.
